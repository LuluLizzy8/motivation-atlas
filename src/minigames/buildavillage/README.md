# Build a Village!

Author: jessicafu232.

## Mechanic

- Choose **Start round**, then **Confirm** for the tutorial or **Skip**.
- Walk with **WASD**, **arrow keys**, or the direction buttons.
- Stand **on a tree or rock** and press **E** to gather.
- Stand **on a named plot** and press **E** to build.
- Complete **all four buildings**. Try both overnight toggle settings.

The contextual action button also gathers and builds for mouse and touch. Build the four plots in any order. Days last 20 seconds and nights last 6 seconds; resource patches recover fully. The tutorial pauses the day clock until your first building is complete; Skip starts it immediately.

**Keep everything overnight** preserves your resources and buildings; switching it off clears them at night. Choose the setting before starting a round.

## Dynamic

When the playtester played the game with keeping inventory toggled on, they took their time with gathering materials; when the playtester played without keeping inventory + buildings, they tried to go as fast as possible to build as many buildings as possible, knowing they would get reset once night hits.

## Aesthetic

The playtester played with the keeping inventory and buildings mode, as well as without the carryover. They called the version with carryover fun. After nightfall wiped resources in the other mode, they reported feeling sad and demotivated, described the game as unbeatable, and said they did not want to keep playing.

## Sources

The game demonstrates Progress, completion and power: "The Hallfords' four reward types: glory, which has no effect on play; sustenance, which lets the avatar keep what it has gained; access, to new places and resources; and facility, new or stronger abilities" (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 24, “Rewards and Schedules”). Sustenance especially is being shown here, with the player keeping what it has gained.

Codex suggested the idea of a time limit per day; this way, we can easily toggle the sustenance feature on or off, by just resetting or maintaining progress each night. I decided to keep this idea, as it felt logical in being a very clear and easily implemented demonstration of the "progress" motivator, and the player can very obviously feel the difference when the toggle is on or off.
