# Golden Sprint | jg5150

Motivator: Challenge.

## Mechanic

Guide Midas through four rooms to the River Pactolus. A runner name is optional; choose **Begin the myth** and click, tap, or press a key to advance the story. Move with **A/D** or **←/→**; jump or swim upward with **W**, **↑**, or **Space**; crouch or dive with **S** or **↓**; attack with **X** or left-click; and use bread with **E** or right-click. On land, bread becomes a brick. In water, it restores nourishment and attacking is disabled. Touch checkpoints, gather treasure and optional wine, avoid enemies and hazards, and reach the gate at the end of each room. On touchscreens, use the on-screen buttons.

Before a run, switch **Hunger drains over time** on or off. With it on, passive nourishment loss rises from room to room. With it off, only enemies and hazards reduce nourishment. Try both settings and compare whether you explore, fight, or rush for the exit. **Fast walkthrough** previews the rooms without scoring, and **Expand game** makes the play area fullscreen. Click or tap inside the game before using keyboard controls.

## Dynamic

Off, one player chased stars and fought for score; another skipped them to finish. On, the first spent longer healing at water, unexpectedly lengthening the run. The second panicked, hit spikes, and dropped bread bricks as stepping stones. Slowing the drain helped them finish. Pressure shifted tactics from reward detours to survival-first decisions.

## Aesthetic

Off, one player felt competitive and wanted to compare scores; the other felt mild panic. On, one felt frustrated by the timer and the other extreme panic. After I slowed the drain, the second still felt panicked but finished. The toggle made the challenge feel manageable off, overwhelming on.

## Sources

The note ties Challenge to “Time pressure and opponent play” (Hunicke et al. 2004, p. 3). Hunger drain adds time pressure while other damage stays fixed.
