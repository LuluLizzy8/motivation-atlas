# glyph-adventure

Motivator: Discovery. Author: Sophia Ling (anonymousanemone)

## Mechanic

Steer the boat with arrow keys while the map is focused, direction buttons,
or taps on adjacent squares. Find the shipwrecked sailor, enter their square,
and bring them to the house. Water and floating plants are passable; land
and the crocodile block movement. Fish and plants reward exploration.

**Fog and mist** on hides unexplored spaces beyond two squares; revealed
spaces stay visible. Off reveals everything within the camera. Switching
settings restarts the same river. Play for a minute or two, without a deadline.

## Dynamic

With Fog and mist on, the playtester tried to collect everything. Even
after seeing the house, they continued exploring, wanting to uncover all
parts of the map before finishing. With it off, they said it was overwhelming to see all of the plants, and fish, and figures, and had to first
get their bearings. Then, they headed straight for the goal without exploring
much. Full visibility discouraged exploration here.

## Aesthetic

With fog off, the player described the many objects on screen as
overwhelming. With it on, they expressed wanting to “unlock” the whole
map and collect everything, even when they knew where to finish. This supports Discovery: hidden spaces invited investigation; full visibility
felt overwhelming.

## Sources

My game demonstrates that "the strategic unveiling of hidden information adds suspense and tension" (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 17) seems to "provoke curiosity"  (Malone 1980, p. 67). As shown by the playtester, who delayed finishing the game to 'collect them all' and reveal all parts of the map, gradually revealing information can inspire someone to explore a lot more than giving them everything all at once at the get-go. 


I used **Codex** to develop the rules, interface, and tests. I was inspired by [Word Game](https://wordgame.cc/) and its  character-grid presentation; and decided to adapt it to a more logographic writing system, hieroglyphs. I kept Codex's suggestion of a making the end goal be about taking a shipwrecked sailor back home (but modified to include shipwrecked sailor references). I rejected its original "journal" system that only stated the items you saw (and not the items you didn't see), and also reported menial things like "a crocodile blocked your way."
