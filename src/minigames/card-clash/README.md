# Card Clash

Motivator: Chance. Author: Annniway026.

## Mechanic

Finish with more points than the bot. You go first. Click, tap, or focus a
legal card and press Enter or Space. Numbers add their value. Undo cancels
the opponent's latest uncanceled number's original points; an existing Double
bonus remains. Double adds your latest uncanceled number's value once more.
Reveal publicly shows one random opposing hand card without taking it.
Defense blocks the next opposing action, not numbers. Shuffle removes itself
and exchanges both remaining hands.

No cards are drawn. Pass only when no legal card exists. When neither side
can act, compare scores; ties draw.

**Randomized Mode** applies only on **New Game**:
- **OFF:** Both sides receive Number 1, Number 5, Number 11, Undo, and Double
  from a separate ten-card scenario. The opponent's starting composition is
  fixed and known; its current hand is not displayed as a live list.
- **ON:** Deal five cards each from a shuffled 29-card pool: two copies of
  1–12 and one of each action. Each hand contains at least three numbers;
  nineteen cards stay unused. The opponent's hand is hidden and its randomly
  assigned number and action cards are initially unknown. Reveal can make
  individual cards public.

The bot policy and scoring stay the same. Both players' scores remain visible
at all times in both modes. This is the constant Competition mechanic.
The toggle varies random starting resources and knowledge of the opponent's
possible actions, rather than changing Competition itself. Chance is the
primary motivator; hidden information also connects to Challenge (Malone).

## Dynamic

During playtesting, Fixed Mode produced a more predictable strategic experience.
The known starting composition let the player anticipate available action types.
Randomized Mode increased uncertainty about both number values and which
functional cards the opponent might hold, encouraging more adaptive decisions.

These observations concern random deals and hidden information. They do not
show that the toggle increases Competition: the opponent, visible scores, and
winning condition are present in both modes. The toggle changes both the deal
and its predictability, so this comparison does not isolate either factor.

## Aesthetic

Fixed Mode felt more systematic and predictable. Randomized Mode created a stronger sense of uncertainty and suspense:
the player considered the possibility of an unexpected action card. The
reported variety and engagement suggest an appeal to replay, not measured
long-term replayability. These are this playtest's reported responses to
Chance and hidden information, not evidence that Competition was switched on
or strengthened.

## Sources

From the course note:
- Competition: "Both players' scores visible at all times, as in Joust"
  (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 20, "Joust"). This describes the scores
  retained in both modes.
- Competition: adversarial play with "clear feedback about who is winning,"
  (Hunicke et al. 2004, p. 3). This remains a valid description of the
  constant score feedback.
- Challenge: "Goals whose outcome is uncertain, through variable difficulty,
  multiple levels of goals, hidden information, or randomness"
  (Malone 1980, pp. 66 to 67). Hidden action-card information is the relevant
  connection; no difficulty adjustment is introduced.
- Chance: "Dice and random card deals" (Malone 1980, p. 52), and randomness
  used "to heighten interest" (p. 67). The randomized initial deal is the
  direct Chance mechanic.
