# Lifeline

Motivator: Fellowship. Author: jace-hong.

## Mechanic

Two players share one screen and one team score: reach 60 within 8 turns, 4 each.

- **Roll** as often as you like; a 2 to 6 adds to your turn total. **Hold** banks it for the team.
- A 1 is a bust: your turn total is lost.
- **Lifeline (the toggle):** on, each player has one lifeline. When you bust, your partner may **Throw lifeline** to save your points, or **Pass**. Off, a bust just loses them.

## Dynamic

With the lifeline on, we both rolled past 12–15 points because a save was waiting. The player passed on rescuing my 9-point bust ("9 points feels too small to waste my only save"), then later rescued my 15-point bust at once and laughed, "Okay, paid you back," after I had saved their 18. With it off, we held around 8–10 and talked less; my 14-point bust drew a sigh. One round each: we won with 64 on, lost with 51 off, so the score gap may be partly luck.

## Aesthetic

On: "It actually felt like we were playing together instead of just waiting for my turn." Off: "It felt like two solo turns glued together." The lifeline turned my bust into the player's decision, which is the push McGonigal describes.

## Sources

Fellowship: "one member's failure pushes the others to do better" (McGonigal 2011, ch. 4), here as a partner's revive.

Built with Claude Code (Anthropic) instead of Codex. Claude suggested auto-focusing Throw lifeline; I changed it to focus neither button, so a rescue is a real choice, not a reflexive Enter, and renamed "Let it go" to "Pass".
