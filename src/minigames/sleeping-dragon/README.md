# Dragon’s Hoard

Motivator: Chance. Author: adit-prabhu.

## Mechanic

Raid a sleeping dragon’s hoard over three trips. **Steal treasure** rolls a
die: 1 wakes the dragon and ends the trip; 2–6 award 5, 10, 15, 20, or 50
gold. **Escape & bank** secures your bag and ends the trip. Banked gold stays
safe.

**Drop carried loot when caught** ON forfeits your bag when caught; OFF keeps
it. Odds and rewards stay identical. Change the checkbox, then press **New
round**. Use mouse, touch, or Tab and Enter/Space. Allow one or two minutes.

## Dynamic

One other person played ON, then OFF. ON, they adopted a four-steal limit:
escaped with 40, woke the dragon immediately, then lost 5 on their second
steal, finishing with 40. OFF, they always stole until caught: 55, 630, and
90 gold after 3, 31, and 4 steals respectively, totaling 775. Removing
forfeiture removed their reason to escape. Different random rolls also
affected scores.

## Aesthetic

ON felt strategic and fun; escaping with 40 felt like a rewarded decision.
OFF lacked thrill: clicking repeatedly and being caught felt inconsequential.
The player found 775 less satisfying than 40. For this player, risking loss
made the reward meaningful.

## Sources

The course note’s Chance section describes Pig as “astonishingly simple,
strategically deep, and increasingly dramatic” (Salen and Zimmerman 2003,
ch. 15, “Pig”). This game adapts that stop-or-risk structure. Codex helped build the game; I retained its suggestion to risk only carried treasure. Scaffolding and DOM helpers come from the course Atlas template; vector art is original.
