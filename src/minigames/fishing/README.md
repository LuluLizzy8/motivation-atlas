# Gone Fishing: when do the bites come?

Motivator: Chance. Author: LuluLizzy.

## Mechanic

You have 24 casts of bait. Press **Cast**, wait a moment for the line, and see whether a fish bites. Keep casting as long as you like, or press **Pack up** to stop early. The row of marks shows every cast so far.

The toggle, **Bites come at random**, changes when fish bite. On, each cast has a 1 in 4 chance. Off, every 4th cast bites, like clockwork. Both catch 6 fish per 24 casts on average. Set it, then press **New round**.

## Dynamic

One playtester played a round with the toggle on, then off. With bites at random, they kept casting to catch more fish than another person and to see how high a catch their luck could reach. With bites on every 4th cast, they spotted the pattern, clicked through the casts as fast as they could, and gave up about halfway through the bait. The same average catch kept them playing one way and made them quit the other. One result I did not expect: chance turned a solo game into a contest, so Competition showed up alongside Chance.

## Aesthetic

With bites at random, they said it felt very random, and that was the fun: hoping for a lucky streak and a bigger catch than someone else's. With bites on every 4th cast, they said there was no point in playing, since they knew every 4th cast would bite, and no point in finishing. The fixed schedule took out the surprise, and the reason to keep casting went with it.

## Sources

From the Chance section of the course note:

- Variable-ratio reinforcement, the slot machine's schedule: "The repeat play of gamblers is strong evidence of the power of variable reinforcement" (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 24, "Rewards and Schedules").

The toggle swaps that variable-ratio schedule for a fixed one with the same average, so any change in how long a player keeps casting comes from the schedule alone.
