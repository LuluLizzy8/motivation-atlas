# Signal Keeper: a moving target

Motivator: Challenge. Author: ghanikhalid.

## Mechanic

Catch the moving signal inside the outlined target. Choose Adaptive
difficulty, then start. Tap/click Lock signal, or focus it and press
Space/Enter. Ten six-second trials allow one attempt each.
Hits earn one point; misses and skipped attempts earn nothing.

With the toggle on, a hit narrows the next target by six percentage points;
a miss widens it by six, within limits of 3% and 60%. With it off, the
target stays 36% wide. Speed, duration, and scoring stay identical.
Restart resets everything; change modes between rounds.

## Dynamic

One other player tested both adaptive versions: 6/10 with the earlier 12%
minimum and 5/10 with the current 3% minimum. With adaptation off, they
scored 10/10.
They reported wanting to keep going or replay for a better score after
misses widened the target. In fixed mode, they described simply pressing
the button ten times.

## Aesthetic

Increasing difficulty excited them. Missing and returning to an easier
target felt frustrating in a motivating way. Adaptation felt like a game;
fixed difficulty felt like a mundane chore, with little sense of response
to their performance despite the displayed hits and misses. The easier
perfect score felt less engaging. Challenge mattered more than success
alone in this player's account.

## Sources

The course note: “Difficulty that rises as the player's ability grows”
(Malone 1980, p. 54). Hits approximate performance; they do not measure
ability. The adaptive target tests this connection to Challenge.
